Where this site stands

This site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Most of it does. This page says which parts I've verified, which parts I haven't, and what to do if you hit something that doesn't work.

I publish this for two reasons. It's the right thing to do — and it's a fair test of whether I'd handle accessibility properly on your project, since a developer who ignores it on their own site will ignore it on yours.

What has been tested

Every page was checked programmatically at 26 viewport widths from 320px to 1920px, covering small phones, large phones, tablets in both orientations, and desktop. Specifically verified:

  • No horizontal scrolling at any tested width, including 320px.
  • Touch targets of at least 24×24 CSS pixels, meeting WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.5.8. Most are 44px or larger.
  • Heading structure — one H1 per page, no skipped levels, headings that describe the section rather than decorate it.
  • Keyboard operation — every interactive element reachable and operable by keyboard, with a visible focus ring that is never suppressed.
  • The mobile menu closes on Escape, and its links are removed from the tab order while closed.
  • Text contrast at 4.5:1 or better for body text against its background; larger text meets or exceeds its lower threshold.
  • Content without JavaScript — every page renders fully readable with scripts disabled.
  • Reduced motion — all animation is disabled for anyone whose system requests it.
  • Form labels — every field has a real associated label, not a placeholder standing in for one.

Specific measures

Motion

Scroll animations are subtle and fire once. Anyone with reduce motion enabled in their operating system gets no animation at all — content simply appears. There is no parallax, no auto-playing video and no carousel that moves on its own.

Colour

The site is deliberately near-monochrome, so no information is conveyed by colour alone. Categories, states and emphasis all carry a text label or a shape difference as well.

Text

Body text is 16px or larger and set in a typeface chosen for legibility. Line length is capped so lines stay comfortable to track. Text can be resized to 200% and reflowed to a 320px viewport without loss of content.

Structure

Landmarks (header, nav, main, footer) are used properly, a skip link is the first focusable element on every page, and the current page is marked in the navigation for screen reader users.

What has not been tested

Being straight about the gaps is more useful than claiming full conformance.

  • Screen readers. Automated checks and correct semantics get most of the way, but I have not yet run a full manual pass with NVDA, JAWS or VoiceOver.
  • Third-party embeds. The scheduling widget on the booking page is supplied by an external provider, and its accessibility is outside my control. If it doesn't work for you, the contact form and direct email reach me the same way.
  • Voice control and switch access. Not yet tested.
  • Cognitive accessibility. Plain language has been a goal throughout, but there has been no formal review.

Before publishing: remove any line above that you have since tested, and add anything new. A statement that overclaims is worse than one that admits gaps — the point of this page is that it's accurate.

Known issues

None currently open.

When one is found it gets listed here with what it affects and when it's expected to be fixed, rather than quietly corrected.

Tell me if something doesn't work

If any part of this site is difficult or impossible to use, email vikas.bh14@gmail.com. Useful to include if you can: the page, what you were trying to do, and what assistive technology or browser you're using.

I'll reply within one business day, and fix genuine barriers as a priority rather than adding them to a backlog. If you need information from this site in a different format, ask and I'll provide it.

Accessibility on client projects

On client work, accessibility is part of the build rather than an add-on quoted separately. Semantic structure, keyboard operability, contrast and labelled forms are the baseline on every project regardless of whether anyone asked.

Where a project has a formal obligation — public sector bodies, healthcare providers, organisations trading in the EU under the European Accessibility Act, or anyone with a documented policy — that gets scoped explicitly, including the testing needed to support the claim.

If accessibility conformance matters to your organisation, raise it on the first call. It changes some structural decisions, and it is far cheaper to build in than to retrofit.
