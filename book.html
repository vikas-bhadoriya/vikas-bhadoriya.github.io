How the call runs

  1. You talk for ten minutes. What the business does, what's wrong or what's needed, what's already been tried.
  2. I ask the awkward questions. Who maintains it after launch, what's the real deadline, what happens if it slips.
  3. You get a straight answer. Rough scope, rough cost, rough timeline — and whether I'm the right person for it.
  4. A written summary follows. Within one business day, so you have something to circulate internally whether or not we work together.

Before the call

Four things
worth having ready.

None of these are required — turn up with nothing and we'll still get somewhere. But having them makes twenty minutes go a great deal further.

The URL

If a site already exists, send the link when you book. I'll have run it through Lighthouse and looked at the setup before we speak.

A rough budget

A range is enough. It's the fastest way to find out whether this is a conversation worth continuing, and neither of us benefits from discovering that in week three.

Who maintains it

Whether someone on your team will edit pages afterwards genuinely changes what gets built. It's the first thing I'll ask.

The real deadline

Not the comfortable one. If there's a trade show, a funding round or a contract date behind it, that shapes everything.

Expectations

What this call
is and isn't.

Booking a call with a freelancer usually means booking a sales pitch. This one is deliberately not that, so here's exactly what you're agreeing to.

What you get

  • A rough cost band on the call, not after a week of deliberation
  • An honest fit assessment — including "this isn't me, try someone who does X"
  • The constraints I can already see, said out loud rather than saved for the proposal
  • A written summary within one business day, yours to keep either way
  • A referral where I know someone better suited

What it isn't

  • A sales call. There's no follow-up sequence and no second call to "circle back"
  • A free audit. Twenty minutes gets impressions, not a diagnosis — that's the rescue audit
  • A commitment. No obligation on either side afterwards
  • A design review. I build what a designer specifies; I don't critique brand work
  • A free consulting hour. If you need detailed technical answers rather than a scoping conversation, that's paid work and I'll say so
