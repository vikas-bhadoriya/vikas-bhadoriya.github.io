The arrangement

Your client
never knows
I exist.

Seven commitments, all of them in the contract rather than just on this page. If any of them is a dealbreaker in reverse — say you'd rather I joined client calls — that's negotiable too.

  • NDA before the first call

    Signed before you tell me anything about the project, not after. Send yours or use mine.

  • I never contact your client

    Not for clarification, not for assets, not after launch. Everything routes through you. If you'd rather I joined calls as a member of your team under your domain email, that works too — your choice, not mine.

  • No credit, no branding, no portfolio use

    Nothing in the footer, no comment in the source, nothing on this site. Portfolio use only with your written permission, and only in the anonymised form used everywhere else here.

  • Your accounts, your infrastructure

    Your repository, your hosting, your staging environment, your project management tool. I work inside your setup rather than asking you to adopt mine.

  • Non-solicit, in writing

    I will not approach your client during the project or after it. This is the fear nobody says out loud, so it goes in the contract as a clause rather than a promise.

  • You are not managing me

    Written scope, a staging link from day one, and an update every Friday whether or not there's good news. If you want daily standups I'll attend. If you want silence until Friday, that's the default.

  • Handover so anyone can take over

    Standard WordPress structure, no proprietary framework, documented custom code. If I became unavailable mid-project your next developer picks it up from the documentation, not from a phone call.

Fit

Worth a call,
or not.

Being specific about both halves saves us a discovery call that goes nowhere. If you're on the right of this list I'll usually know someone who is a better fit.

Send me this

  • Elementor builds from a Figma, XD or PSD file — rebuilt to spec, not approximated
  • WooCommerce with awkward requirements — verification steps, subscriptions, digital delivery, multi-currency
  • Performance rescue on a build that's failing Core Web Vitals before a client sees the report
  • Custom plugins and PHP where your team stops and the requirement doesn't
  • Migrations — host moves, domain changes, DNS and redirects, no downtime
  • Overflow capacity when you've sold more than you can staff this month
  • Multilingual builds for DACH, French or pan-European clients

Send this elsewhere

  • Shopify, Webflow, Wix, Squarespace — WordPress only, and I'd rather say so than learn on your budget
  • Design work — I build what your designer specifies, I don't originate it
  • Copywriting — I'll tell you what each section needs, not write it
  • Full-time embedded resource — I run 2–3 projects at a time, not one client exclusively
  • A deadline that's already impossible — you'll hear that on the first call, not in week four
  • Frontend-heavy React or Next.js builds — I'll do the WordPress and API side, your team owns the frontend

Straight answers

The awkward
questions.

You're considering putting your reputation in the hands of someone you've never met. These are the four things you'd want to ask and probably won't.

Will you poach our client?

No, and there's a non-solicit clause rather than just my word. But the commercial reason is more convincing than the legal one: agency work is a standing share of my income, and doing this once would end all of it. My direct pipeline comes from search and writing, not from other people's client lists.

What if you disappear mid-project?

Work lives in your repository on your infrastructure, using standard WordPress structure and no proprietary framework, with custom code documented as it's written. There's no point at which the project only exists on my machine.

That isn't a promise about my reliability — it's a property of how the work is set up, which is the only kind of answer worth anything here.

Will the quality embarrass us?

Judge it before committing. Nineteen live projects are documented here, several for clients in regulated sectors where the work was reviewed by people whose job is finding faults. Or start with a four-hour paid task and see.

I'd rather you tested me on something small than took a reference's word for it.

How much of our time will this cost?

Less than a junior in-house developer. You get a written scope before work starts, a staging link from day one, and a Friday update. What I need from you is decisions and assets on the dates in the scope — everything else is mine.

The projects that go wrong go wrong because feedback stalls, so the scope names who owes what and when.
