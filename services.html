Scope

What I
don't do.

Listed because a clear no saves us both a call, and because anyone claiming to do everything is claiming to be good at nothing.

For most of these I can recommend someone who does it properly.

  • Brand identity and logo design. I build what a designer specifies. I'm not the person to invent your visual identity.
  • Copywriting. I'll tell you what each section needs to say and how long it should be. Writing it well is a different profession.
  • Paid ads and SEO retainers. Technical SEO is part of every build. Campaign management and link building are not services I offer.
  • Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow. WordPress only. A specialist who's honest about their platform is worth more than a generalist.
  • Projects under about $1,500. Below that I can't do the discovery and testing properly, and a rushed build costs you more later.
  • Deadlines that are already impossible. If the date can't be met properly I'll say so on the first call rather than agree and disappoint you.

Questions

Asked often
enough to answer.

If yours isn't here, ask it on a call — twenty minutes, and I'll tell you if I'm the wrong fit.

How much does a WordPress website cost?

Most business sites land between $600 and $4,000. Commerce and custom development runs $4,000 to $12,000. The variables that move a project between bands are the number of unique templates, whether custom development is needed, how many languages, and whether there are compliance or integration requirements. Every project is quoted against a written scope with a fixed price before work starts.

How long does a project take?

Four to six weeks for a business site, six to twelve for commerce or custom work. That assumes content and feedback arrive roughly when agreed — the single biggest cause of overrun is waiting on material from the client side, so the written scope names what's needed and when.

Do you work with agencies as a subcontractor?

Yes, and several of the projects in the portfolio were delivered that way. NDA as standard, no direct contact with your client, work delivered under your brand, invoiced to you. Rates, capacity and terms are here.

Who owns the website when it's finished?

You do — code, content, credentials and hosting account, all transferred at handover along with documentation and a recorded walkthrough. Nothing is licensed back to you, nothing is hosted on my account, and there's no arrangement that makes leaving difficult.

Do you use page builders or write everything from scratch?

Both, depending on who maintains the site afterwards. Elementor when your team needs to edit layouts themselves — built lean, with custom PHP widgets instead of addon packs. A custom theme when the site has unusual requirements or when nobody on your side will ever touch the layout.

The honest version: a page builder built carelessly is slow, and a custom theme nobody can edit is a different kind of expensive. The choice depends on your team, not on ideology.

What happens if something breaks after launch?

Bugs in what I built are fixed at no charge — that's not a warranty period, it's just how it works. Beyond that, a care plan covers updates, monitoring and an agreed number of hours each month. Without one, I'll still respond, just not on a guaranteed timeline.

Can you work with our existing designer?

That's the arrangement I prefer. Give me a Figma, XD or PSD file and I'll rebuild it to spec, flagging anything that won't behave as drawn on mobile before I start rather than after.

You're in India — how does that work across time zones?

There's roughly four hours of daily overlap with the UK and Europe, and early-morning overlap with the US East Coast. In practice most work happens asynchronously with a written update every Friday, and calls are scheduled in your working hours rather than mine. Nine countries' worth of projects have run this way.
