Where stores break

Three problems
plugins don't solve.

Most WooCommerce trouble isn't the platform. It's a product or a market that doesn't match the assumptions the defaults were built on — and no combination of plugins reconciles that.

The product is configurable

A base unit, optional modules, retrofit parts and spares. Variations don't model it, and a customer who orders the wrong combination becomes a refund and a support call.

Something must happen before the sale

Age verification, identity checks, trade-account approval, licence validation. Bolted on afterwards it's a leak; wired into the flow it just works.

Six plugins are fighting

Each one solved a problem, and together they've made a checkout nobody understands. Every update is a risk, and the store slows down a little more each month.

What gets built

Seven areas,
most builds.

Which of these you need depends on the product. The scope names them explicitly, so nothing arrives as an unexpected line item.

  • A product model that matches reality

    Before any design work, how your catalogue is actually structured — variations, bundles, configurable systems, spares. Getting this wrong is the mistake that costs most later, because everything else is built on top of it.

  • A checkout built for your sale

    Fields your business needs and none it doesn't, verification steps in the right order, and a flow tested on a phone. Every extra field costs conversion, so each one has to justify itself.

  • Payments and tax for the markets you serve

    Gateway integration, multi-currency, and VAT or sales-tax handling that reflects where you actually ship — including EU VAT rules and reverse charge where they apply.

  • Verification and compliance steps

    Age gating, identity verification, trade-account approval. Built into the purchase flow rather than as a modal that a determined visitor can dismiss.

  • Fulfilment that fits your operation

    Shipping rules, zones and rates, digital delivery, subscription renewals, and export to whatever your warehouse or accounts team already uses.

  • Performance, because stores are heavy

    Catalogue query optimisation, cart fragment handling, and image work. A slow store loses sales in a way a slow brochure site doesn't — the cost is directly measurable.

  • An admin your team can run

    Order management, stock, and content editing that doesn't require a developer. Plus documentation for the three things that will inevitably go wrong at 6pm on a Friday.

Questions

Asked before
every store.

If yours isn't here, twenty minutes on a call will usually settle it.

WooCommerce or Shopify?

Shopify if you want a standard store running quickly and are happy inside its rules. It's genuinely good at that, and I'd say so rather than talk you out of it.

WooCommerce when the product or the flow doesn't fit those rules — configurable products, verification steps, unusual tax, or logic Shopify charges an app subscription for and still doesn't quite do. You also own it outright, with no transaction fee and no platform deciding what your checkout may contain.

Can you handle EU VAT and multi-currency?

Yes — it's a routine requirement for the European clients in the portfolio. Destination-based VAT, reverse charge for B2B, VAT number validation, and prices displayed inclusive or exclusive depending on the market's convention.

Worth saying plainly: I set the system up correctly, but I'm not a tax adviser. Your accountant confirms the rates and the rules; I make the store apply them.

How many plugins will the store need?

Fewer than most. Where a well-maintained plugin does the job, I use it. Where the choice is between four plugins fighting each other and one purpose-built custom plugin, custom wins — it's cheaper over three years and far less likely to break on update.

Can you migrate our existing store?

Yes, including products, customers, order history and URL structure with redirects. Migrations from Shopify, Magento or an older WooCommerce install are all workable — quoted separately since the effort depends entirely on the data quality on the way out.

Will our team be able to manage it?

That's a build requirement, not an afterthought. Adding products, editing content, processing orders and managing stock are all designed for non-technical staff, with documentation and a recorded walkthrough at handover.

What about payment security and PCI?

Card data goes directly to a compliant gateway and never touches your server, which keeps you in the simplest PCI category. I build to that model, keep the store on HTTPS with current software, and document the setup — but formal certification is a process your payment provider handles, not something I can grant.

Can you add subscriptions or memberships?

Yes — recurring billing, renewal handling, failed-payment recovery and member-only content. It's a meaningful addition to scope rather than a checkbox, so it gets quoted explicitly.

What happens after launch?

I watch the first days of real orders closely, because that's when edge cases surface. Bugs in what I built are fixed at no charge. Beyond that, stores benefit more than most sites from a care plan — a broken checkout costs money by the hour.
